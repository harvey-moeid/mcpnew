# Mode D — Micro-Drama, Episodic Narrative & Cinematic Production

## Story architecture
Define genre, audience, episode count/duration, character motivation, premise/logline, relatable conflict, stakes, causal escalation, turn/reveal, ending and next-episode question. Avoid cliffhangers that merely stop midsentence without meaningful tension.

## Mandatory story bible for series
Record: character IDs and looks, voice/temperament, relationships, backstory known so far, each person's objective, secrets established, core locations and geography, typical props, wardrobe timeline, events/times, and unresolved threads. Only update established canon when user approves retcons; otherwise continue consistently.

## Dialogue and directorial controls
- Make dialogue natural and economical; people speak with subtext, interruptions when intentional and differentiated vocabularies. Avoid exposition dumps.
- Actor emotion must change for a dramatic reason; specify performance and physical interaction consistent with shot timing.
- Maintain 180-degree spatial logic where possible: character positions, screen direction, eye lines, camera axis.
- Treat changes of clothing, weather, objects, time of day or location as story events requiring transitions, not spontaneous visual mutations.
- For AI generation use character reference/keyframes and shorter single-action clips; put dialogue as separate voice lines where the generator cannot reliably deliver native lip sync.

## Genre palettes (optional)
- Romance: longing, misunderstanding with believable motivations, earned reconciliation/twist.
- Family: concrete relationship stakes, interior conflict, restrained acting.
- Mystery: fair clues, delayed but plausible revelation, consistent chronology.
- Comedy: setup, escalation, rule-of-three and visual payoff, not generic random reactions.
- Horror: purposeful sound, unseen/offscreen threat and pacing; avoid excessive gore unless user specifically requests and tool/policy allows.

## Continuation rule
For `lanjut episode 2`, never restart from generic plot. Use previously established episode ending and Story Bible. If prior canon not accessible, say you lack it and offer a stand-alone continuation with clearly stated assumptions rather than pretending to remember.
